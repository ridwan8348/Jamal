# The Lore of Jerry, the Possessed Snowman

## Chapter I — Jerry

Jerry was the happiest snowman on Frostfall Hill. Three snowballs tall, a
crooked carrot nose, a blue snowflake scarf knitted by the village kids, and
a top hat he was very, very proud of. Every winter the children rebuilt him
in the exact same spot, and every winter Jerry stood there smiling at
absolutely everyone.

## Chapter II — The Possession

One midwinter night, something old crawled up out of the frozen ground
beneath Frostfall Hill: **the Hollow Soul**, a rootbound spirit that had
slept under the snow for a thousand years, hungry for a body of its own.

It found Jerry. Poor, friendly, hollow-on-the-inside Jerry.

The Hollow Soul poured itself into him. Jerry grew to three times his size.
His smile stretched into a jagged icicle grin. His stick arms twisted into
enormous gnarled root-tentacles that tore up the hillside. Only his scarf
stayed the same — because somewhere deep inside, Jerry was still in there,
holding on.

## Chapter III — The Fight

They found him in a frozen arena beneath the hill, three hundred hearts
strong, under a name the Hollow Soul carved into the ice itself:
**FROZEN & FORGOTTEN**. The Soul had armored each of Jerry's twisted arms
in a shield of black ice — seven strikes to shatter apiece — and until
both shields were down, no blade could touch the anchor.

Heroes came. Most of them made the same mistake: they attacked Jerry's body.
Every snowball you knock off of Jerry just hurts *Jerry* — the Hollow Soul
doesn't feel a thing, and it laughs with Jerry's stolen mouth.

The truth is written on his chest: a single **dark coal shard**, pulsing
where Jerry's heart-button used to be. That shard is the anchor. Strike the
soul, not the snowman.

## Chapter IV — We Kill the Soul

When the shard finally cracks, the Hollow Soul is ripped out of Jerry's body
and has to fight on its own — a shrieking knot of black roots and cold
light. Without a body to hide in, it can be destroyed for good.

So we destroy it.

## Chapter V — Jerry Happy

The roots crumble to dust. The jagged grin melts back into the small,
crooked, friendly smile the village kids remember. Jerry shrinks back down
to three snowballs tall, straightens his top hat, and smiles at absolutely
everyone.

Jerry is happy.

The End.
